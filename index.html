About

Hello, I'm Daniel.

I am a PhD Candidate and Graduate Assistant in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV), working in the Software Evolution (EVOL) Lab under the supervision of Professor John Businge. I hold an MSc in Computer Science from Makerere University, Uganda (advised by Dr. Joyce Nakatumba-Nabende and Prof. Michel R.V. Chaudron) and a BSc in Computer Science from Gulu University, Uganda. My research lies at the intersection of Empirical Software Engineering and Agentic AI, with publications in venues including ASE, EMSE, MSR, SCAM, and AIware. I also serve the research community on the program and artifact evaluation committees of top software engineering venues, including ESEC/FSE, ASE, ISSTA, ICSME, and MSR, and as a reviewer for journals such as ACM TOCE, Scientific Reports, and Data in Brief.

Research

What I work on

Research agenda

AI Coding Agents & LLMs

How autonomous agents such as Copilot, Devin, and Cursor write, submit, and conflict over code in real repositories, and how developers actually adopt AI-generated patches.

Learn more

Patch Integration Across Forks

Making bug fixes propagate reliably across forks that have drifted apart through independent refactoring.

Learn more

Software Ecosystems

Treating software as interconnected networks to understand how dependency structure creates systemic risk.

Learn more

Code Smells & Quality

Where design smells appear, why they persist, and what roles they play as software evolves.
